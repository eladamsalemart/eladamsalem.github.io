## 1. Global art market 2025/2026: size, online share, price segments, galleries

### Takeaway
The global art market grew back to about $59.6B in 2025 (+4%), the first growth since 2022, but it is still below its pre-pandemic level. Online-only sales fell to their lowest level since 2019, and buying moved back to in-person channels (fairs and galleries). Volume is concentrated in low price points: roughly 85 to 96% of transactions are under $50K. So the market is in-person and low-ticket, which suits a discovery app that sends people to physical shows. It is a weaker fit for a "sell online" thesis.

### Cited Findings
- 2025 global art sales rose 4% to an estimated **$59.6B**, the first year of growth since 2022 (Art Basel & UBS Art Market Report 2026, Arts Economics / Clare McAndrew, published 12 Mar 2026) — [Art Basel story](https://www.artbasel.com/stories/the-art-basel-and-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026); [The Art Newspaper](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/03/12/art-basel-ubs-art-market-report-2025-four-percent-growth) (headline: "remain below pre-pandemic levels")
- 2025 dealer sales rose 2% to **$34.8B**. Public auction sales rose 9% to $20.7B. Private auction sales fell 4% to just under $4.2B — [Malay Mail / press release syndication](https://www.malaymail.com/news/money/mediaoutreach/2026/03/12/the-art-basel-and-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026-global-art-sales-rose-4-to-usd-596-billion-in-2025/453902); [Artlyst](https://artlyst.com/art-basel-and-ubs-global-market-report-2026-highlights-growth/)
- 2025 online sales fell to **$9.2B**, the lowest level since 2019. One summary reports online share falling by 3 points to about **15%** of the market — [Hypebeast](https://hypebeast.com/2026/3/art-basel-and-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026); [press release PDF](https://d2u3kfwd92fzu7.cloudfront.net/Press_Release_l_The_Art_Basel_and_UBS_Global_Art_Market_Report_2026_I_E.pdf). **CONFLICT:** a UBS page shows "online-only sales fell to USD 11.0B, −17% from 2021 peak of $13.3B". That figure probably comes from an older edition mixed into the page — [UBS art market research](https://www.ubs.com/global/en/our-firm/art/art-market-research.html). Use $9.2B (2025).
- Dealers reported a notable decline in the share of their sales made exclusively online. Fairs rose to **35%** of dealer sales in 2025, up from 31% in 2024 — [Art Basel 7 trends](https://www.artbasel.com/stories/art-basel-and-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026-art-market-trends); [Artinfoland](https://magazine.artinfoland.com/the-art-basel-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026/)
- In 2024, online channels were **33%** of dealer sales, or 37% including fair Online Viewing Rooms. Most of these online sales went through dealers' own websites, email and social media (Art Basel/UBS 2025 report, via Artlogic) — [Hyperallergic / Artlogic](https://hyperallergic.com/what-2025-art-basel-ubs-report-means-dealers-artlogic-breaks-it-down)
- Smallest dealers (turnover under $250K), 2025:
  - Local collectors took 71% of their private-collector sales, up 9 points.
  - 48% expected better sales, up from 31% in 2024.
  - Buyer counts fell most sharply in this segment.
  - Sources: [press release](https://d2u3kfwd92fzu7.cloudfront.net/Press_Release_l_The_Art_Basel_and_UBS_Global_Art_Market_Report_2026_I_E.pdf); [MyArtBroker](https://www.myartbroker.com/value/articles/art-basel-ubs-art-market-report-2026); [Artinfoland](https://magazine.artinfoland.com/the-art-basel-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026/)
- Gallery openings vs closures: the 2026 report says new gallery openings outpaced closures overall, despite high-profile exits — [Artinfoland](https://magazine.artinfoland.com/the-art-basel-ubs-global-art-market-report-2026/). The 2025 report devoted a section to countering narratives about gallery closures — [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/market/art-dealer-sales-2025-2754173)
- Sub-$50K segment: about **85%** of transactions in 2024 were under $50K — [Rob Fields substack summary](https://iamrobfields.substack.com/p/is-the-art-world-getting-better-at). Another summary puts the share at 89–96% over two decades, with $1M+ lots under 0.5% in 2025 — [MyArtBroker](https://www.myartbroker.com/value/articles/art-basel-ubs-art-market-report-2026). **CONFLICT / definitional:** these may be auction-lot shares, not dealer transactions. Check against the full report.
- Gallery economics, 2025 survey of about 1,650 gallerists (Art Basel/UBS 2025 report, via Artnet):
  - Total costs rose about 5% on average.
  - Payroll, rent and art fairs are the biggest expenses.
  - Fairs are roughly **one third** of dealers' total spending.
  - Source: [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/market/art-dealer-sales-2025-2754173)
- Artlogic Gallery Report 2025 (gallerists in 50+ countries):
  - **63%** say running a gallery is harder than 2 years ago.
  - Only about 25% blame rising operating costs.
  - Economic uncertainty and weak collector demand are the main pressures.
  - Most revenue still comes from existing collectors.
  - Source: [Artlogic Gallery Report 2025 PDF](https://info.artlogic.net/hubfs/The%20Artlogic%20Gallery%20Report%202025/The%20Artlogic%20Gallery%20Report%202025.pdf?hsLang=en)
- Art fair costs:
  - First Thursday survey of 56 galleries: **46%** spent over £30K (about $40K) to attend a single fair, and 31% plan fewer fairs — [ARTnews](https://www.artnews.com/art-news/market/new-2025-survey-galleries-art-fair-model-first-thursday-1234749459)
  - NADA NY booths cost $11,000 for 120–150 sq ft, or $3,500 for "Projects". Younger-gallery "Focus" booths cost $11,825–$14,795 (2026) — [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/what-does-a-booth-cost-at-a-new-york-art-fair/)
  - Small-gallery cost at Art Basel Miami Beach was $120K to $275K+ all-in (older Artnet survey) — [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/market/art-fair-economics-smallgalleries-gamble-989555)
  - Shipping to major fairs averaged $15K–45K — [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/market/art-fair-alternatives-analysis-1097516)
  - Artsy (Feb 2025) interviewed a dozen small gallerists on fair costs — [Artsy via Capsule](https://capsuleshanghai.com/press/235-artsy-how-much-art-fairs-really-cost/)
  - Artsy Art Fair Report 2024: galleries doing fewer fairs cite cost as the main reason — [Artsy PDF](https://pages.artsy.net/rs/609-FDY-207/images/The%20Artsy%20Art%20Fair%20Report%202024.02.24pdf.pdf?version=0)
- Hiscox Online Art Trade Report:
  - The latest edition found was **2023** (2022 data): online art sales of $10.8B (+6%), 15.9% of all art sales.
  - 30% of online buyers planned to cut back.
  - Source: [Hiscox press release](https://www.hiscoxgroup.com/news/press-releases/2023/26-04-23)
  - The 2022 edition reported that 47% of new buyers made their first purchase online — [ArtTactic](https://arttactic.com/?p=61672)

### Inferences
- One fair booth costs a small gallery $4K–$40K+, and fairs are about a third of a gallery's costs. A ₪590 (about $160) tour per exhibition is negligible next to that. The pitch should frame the tour as "extending the exhibition's life and reach" next to fair and opening costs, not as a cost centre.
- Buying is moving back to in-person channels, and small dealers rely on local collectors (71%). Both support a local, Israel-first "drive footfall to shows" value proposition more than an e-commerce one.
- 63% of galleries say business is harder. Galleries will be price-sensitive and will want measurable ROI (visits, follows, enquiries).

### Gaps
- The full Art Basel/UBS 2026 PDF could not be opened. Still unconfirmed: the exact primary vs secondary split, the number of active galleries worldwide, the 2025 closure and opening counts, and the exact share of dealer sales under $50K.
- No **Hiscox Online Art Trade Report 2025 or 2026** was found in search. The series may have paused after 2023. This was not confirmed.
- Marketing spend as a % of gallery budget was not found.

## 2. Artists' economics and tools

### Takeaway
Visual artists earn very little from their art. The UK median is about £12,500 a year from all art activity, and more than half earn under £5K from sales. Artists rely heavily on Instagram, and marketplaces take 35–40% commission. Willingness to pay for a subscription is real but small. ₪29 a month (about $8) is in line with the low end of artist tools.

### Cited Findings
- UK (DACS-commissioned survey, 2024, 1,200+ visual artists): median annual income from artistic practice is **£12,500**, down 40% since 2010 — [University of Glasgow](https://www.gla.ac.uk/news/headline_1130637_en.html); [The Art Newspaper](https://theartnewspaper.com/2024/11/25/uk-artists-earning-as-little-as-£7500-per-year-says-new-report) (headline: some earn as little as £7,500)
- A separate 2025 UK survey of 271 artists (2024 earnings) found the same £12,500 median. **56%** earned under £5,000 from art sales — [Creative United, UK artists' livelihoods 2024 survey](https://www.creativeunited.org.uk/services/understanding-uk-artists-livelihoods-the-2024-survey/)
- Ireland: Visual Artists Ireland published an Artists' Livelihoods Report (Nov 2025) — [VAI PDF](https://visualartists.ie/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2025.11.27-Artists-Livelihoods-Report_Final.pdf). Figures were not extracted.
- Finland: Artists' Association of Finland ran a 2025 member survey on visual artists' income and unemployment — [artists.fi](https://www.artists.fi/en/advocacyj/member-survey-2025). Figures were not extracted.
- Germany: BBK and Stiftung Kunstfonds ran a survey (Prognos AG, Feb–Apr 2025), with a final report due 22 Sep 2025 — [BBK Sachsen-Anhalt](https://www.bbk-sachsenanhalt.de/en/?p=28003). The median was not found.
- Marketplace commissions:
  - Saatchi Art takes **40%** on originals. Artfinder takes 35–42% depending on tier (2026 platform guide) — [Price Your Painting](https://priceyourpainting.substack.com/p/where-to-sell-your-paintings-in-2026)
  - **CONFLICT:** an Alibaba-hosted page claims 25% and 15%. It is low quality and should be disregarded — [Alibaba smartbuy](https://smartbuy.alibaba.com/best-selling/best-sites-to-sell-artwork)
- Instagram as a discovery and buying channel:
  - An older Hiscox survey found 63% of buyers name Instagram as their preferred platform to find art.
  - An older Art Basel/UBS survey found 61% of HNW collectors had bought art they first saw on Instagram.
  - Source: [The Art Newspaper, "Instagram turns ten" (2020)](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2020/10/14/instagram-turns-ten-how-the-worlds-favourite-photo-app-disrupted-the-art-market)
- Direct-to-artist channels: in the 2025 Survey of Global Collecting, direct-from-artist purchases doubled year-on-year to **20%** of HNW art spending — [artcollectornews summary](https://artcollectornews.com/post/the-new-art-class-how-women-gen-z-and-digital-platforms-are-redefining-collecting-in-2025)

### Inferences
- Median art income is £12.5K a year, about ₪58K or ₪4.8K a month. ₪29 a month is about 0.6% of that. It is affordable, but artists will drop it unless it brings visible exposure or sales.
- Direct-to-artist buying is rising, and Instagram is the default. The artist pitch should be "a portfolio and studio journal that collectors in Israel actually see, alongside Instagram", not a replacement for it.

### Gaps
- A US visual artist median was not found in this round. A candidate source is the BLS OES "Fine Artists" category, which was not retrieved.
- There is no solid quantitative source on **Instagram organic reach decline** for artists. Only anecdotal coverage exists.
- Not retrieved: Artwork Archive pricing (about $6.50–$15 a month in earlier knowledge, unverified here), Squarespace/Wix prices, and open-call fee levels.

## 3. Israel: galleries, artists, fairs, museums, war impact

### Takeaway
Tel Aviv has a strong anchor audience. The Tel Aviv Museum of Art had about 1.0M visits in 2025 and is ranked #77 worldwide. Fresh Paint draws "tens of thousands", claimed at over 40K. The commercial and independent gallery sector is under pressure from three directions: war (from Oct 2023), international boycotts, and Ministry of Culture budget cuts. Five cooperative galleries were at risk in May 2026. There is no reliable public count of Israeli galleries or working visual artists. This is a real data gap.

### Cited Findings
- **Tel Aviv Museum of Art visits:**
  - 2023: 906,350
  - 2024: **1,057,362**
  - 2025: **1,000,096**, ranked #77 in The Art Newspaper's most-visited list
  - Sources: [Tel Aviv municipality, museum visits 2025 (PDF)](https://www.tel-aviv.gov.il/Transparency/DocLib13/ביקורים%20במוזיאונים%202025.pdf); [Time Out TLV](https://timeout.co.il/מוזיאון-תל-אביב-מבקרים-26/); [Maariv](https://www.maariv.co.il/hamekomon/culture/article-992896)
- Other Tel Aviv museums (2024, municipal table): Eretz Israel Museum about 408K visits, ANU Museum of the Jewish People about 241K — [Tel Aviv municipality PDF](https://www.tel-aviv.gov.il/Transparency/DocLib13/ביקורים%20במוזיאונים%202025.pdf)
- During the war (Iran escalation, March 2026), the Tel Aviv Museum was closed to the general public on Home Front Command instructions and ran small, registered group activities near protected spaces — [TAMA emergency action plan](https://www.tamuseum.org.il/he/emergency-response-action-plan/)
- TAMA director (JPost, 2025–26):
  - "We can't bring exhibitions from abroad because everyone boycotts us."
  - The museum gets 45% of its funding from the municipality and 5% or less from the Ministry of Culture.
  - Tourism has collapsed.
  - Source: [Jerusalem Post, "War, boycotts and shrinking budgets push Israeli culture to the brink"](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/culture/article-902692)
- Boycott pressure:
  - Open letter (Sep 2025) urging Judy Chicago to cancel her TAMA show — [The Art Newspaper](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/09/22/dont-be-complicit-open-letter-calls-on-judy-chicago-and-pussy-riots-nadya-tolakonnikova-to-cancel-tel-aviv-exhibition)
  - The show went ahead — [JTA](https://www.jta.org/2025/11/21/israel/judy-chicagos-feminist-art-lands-in-tel-aviv-igniting-a-boycott-call-and-hard-questions-about-israel)
  - #VisualArtsforPalestine campaign — [ArtReview](https://artreview.com/artists-denounce-israeli-apartheid-renew-calls-for-boycott/)
- **Cooperative galleries at risk (Haaretz, 18 May 2026):**
  - Five Tel Aviv cooperative galleries face gradual closure after changes to the Ministry of Culture's support criteria: Hanina, Binyamin, Alfred, Indie and P8.
  - Source: [Haaretz (paywalled)](https://www.haaretz.co.il/gallery/art/2026-05-18/ty-article/.premium/0000019e-3bb6-d222-a1be-7bb685590000)
- Early war (Oct–Nov 2023): Israeli buying of art largely stopped, and money went to philanthropy. Tirosh auction house CEO Amitai Hazan expected negative effects — [israeliart.co.il](https://israeliart.co.il/איך-משפיעה-המלחמה-על-שוק-האמנות-המקומי). This is a trade blog, so treat it as anecdotal.
- Structural point (older, about 2018): most Israeli art is traded on the secondary market, and Israeli works hold value locally but little abroad — [Globes](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001263496)
- **Fresh Paint 2025 (צבע טרי):**
  - Dates and venue: 21–26 May 2025, Kremnitzer Technical Center, Tel Aviv.
  - Exhibitors: about 40 galleries and curatorial projects, 48 artists in the "Greenhouse" (חממה) section, and 44 design exhibitors — [Portfolio](https://www.prtfl.co.il/archives/222671)
  - Attendance: sponsor Harel describes "tens of thousands of visitors" — [Harel](https://www.harel-group.co.il/service/Pages/art-club/freshpaint.aspx). Portfolio's open call says the fair attracts "over 40,000 visitors a week" (general claim, not audited) — [Portfolio](https://www.prtfl.co.il/archives/218660)
- Art school graduation shows:
  - Bezalel's annual graduate show (new Jerusalem campus, likely 2024) presented about **400 graduates** across all departments — [Maariv](https://www.maariv.co.il/culture/theater-art/Article-1027809)
  - Shenkar, HaMidrasha (Kfar Saba; MFA show at Yarkon 19 TLV) and Bezalel MFA (TLV) run summer graduate shows that Time Out covers each year — [Time Out](https://timeout.co.il/הכי-טרי-שיש-בוגרי-בתי-הספר-לאמנות-מציגי/)
- Knesset Research Center paper on benefits for artists: visual artists get a specific pension supplement and income-guarantee support. It is older, and the figures were unreadable in extraction — [Knesset MMM PDF](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/globaldocs/MMM/674e6b58-e9f7-e411-80c8-00155d010977/2_674e6b58-e9f7-e411-80c8-00155d010977_11_10557.pdf)

### Inferences
- Visit volumes give a sense of the demand pool in Tel Aviv: TAMA alone has about 1M visits a year, plus Fresh Paint's tens of thousands. The addressable paying gallery base, however, is small, probably tens to low hundreds of galleries and project spaces (unverified; see Gaps).
- Five cooperative galleries are losing state support. That is a risk (less budget) and also an opening: a cheap, compelling way to document and extend shows.
- The boycott-driven drop in foreign shows and tourism makes local audiences and digital reach abroad (3D tours) more valuable to Israeli institutions.
- Graduation shows run once a year, are short, and feature hundreds of graduates (about 400 at Bezalel). They are a natural fit for automated phone-photo 3D documentation, which suggests per-school or per-department packages.

### Gaps
- **Number of galleries in Tel Aviv or Israel:** no authoritative count found. Possible sources: Ministry of Culture support lists, Gallery Weekend listings, Time Out's gallery index.
- **Number of working visual artists in Israel** and their income: not found. The CBS occupational tables and the Artists' Union (איגוד האמנים) did not surface.
- **CBS Social Survey culture-consumption module** (share visiting museums or exhibitions): not retrieved. The CBS survey exists, and culture is covered in "Israel in Figures", but the specific % was not found.
- Tel Aviv Gallery Weekend (סוף שבוע גלריות) participation and attendance: not found.
- No quantified data on Israeli gallery sales during 2024–2026.

## 4. Visitors and audiences: who goes, how they discover shows

### Takeaway
Little recent quantitative data surfaced. Older Culture Track findings show younger audiences discover culture through social media and friends and attend for fun and social reasons, while older audiences rely more on newspapers. The biggest barrier is the feeling that a place is "not for someone like me". These points support a social, follow-based discovery app.

### Cited Findings
- Culture Track 2017: the top motivation for cultural participation is "having fun" (81%). The biggest barrier is the perception that it is "not for someone like me" — [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/culture-track-2017-report-summary-1120559?amp=1)
- Culture Track 2014 (Boston edition): social media plays a much more important role in finding cultural activities, but newspapers still matter for older audiences. Young audiences go to socialize with friends — [WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/news/2014/09/26/culture-track-2014); [MFA Boston](https://www.mfa.org/node/2124)
- Culture Track 2014 national survey: 4,026 respondents — [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/123030/study-finds-us-cultural-consumers-are-social-and-promiscuous/)

### Inferences
- An Israel-first app should target the "not for me" barrier. Friendly, social discovery of exhibitions (a map, follows, short studio clips) addresses it directly.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved in this round: Culture Track 2024/2025, Art Fund, Audience Agency (UK), and mobile-at-exhibition statistics.
- No Hebrew source was found on the demographics of Israeli gallery-goers.

## 5. Collectors: discovery and buying

### Takeaway
High-net-worth (HNW) collectors are young-skewed in the surveys. In the 2025 survey, 74% are millennials or Gen Z. These collectors buy across digital and social channels, and direct-from-artist purchases are rising. Dealers and galleries remain the main channel.

### Cited Findings
- Survey of Global Collecting 2025: about 3,100 HNW collectors in 10 markets, with **74%** millennials or Gen Z — [Art Basel](https://www.artbasel.com/stories/the-art-basel-and-ubs-survey-of-global-collecting-2025); [PDF](https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/download/The-Art-Basel-and-UBS-Survey-of-Global-Collecting-in-2025.pdf)
- Dealers accounted for **43%** of HNW art spending. Direct-from-artist purchases doubled to 20%. **51%** bought at least one work via Instagram or other social platforms, often without seeing it in person. This attribution is mixed between the 2025 and 2026 editions and needs checking — [artcollectornews](https://artcollectornews.com/post/the-new-art-class-how-women-gen-z-and-digital-platforms-are-redefining-collecting-in-2025)
- Gen Z spends more than twice as much on art as other generations (2025 survey) — [Artsy](https://www.artsy.net/article/artsy-editorial-gen-spends-twice-art-generations-art-basel-ubs-report-finds)
- Wealthiest collectors increasingly use AI for research — [ARTnews](https://www.artnews.com/art-news/market/wealthiest-collectors-rely-ai-art-basel-ubs-report-1234801457/)
- Survey of Global Collecting 2026: family influence is the most common route into collecting (28%), rising to 40% among Gen Z — [UBS](https://www.ubs.com/global/en/our-firm/art/art-market-research.html)
- Hiscox 2022: 31% of young collectors bought their first artwork online, up from 14% in 2020. A later edition found only 18% of younger online buyers planned a purchase, down from 44% — [ArtTactic/Hiscox](https://arttactic.com/?p=61672)

### Inferences
- Collectors already buy from artists directly and through social media. A curated, local, follow-based feed with real exhibition context is a credible complement to Instagram, which could support artist subscription value.
- These surveys cover HNW buyers. Israeli entry-level buyers (under ₪20K) are not represented, which is a gap.

### Gaps
- No data on Israeli collectors (size of base, spend) was found.

## 6. Institutions, museums, municipalities, art schools: willingness to pay for documentation

### Takeaway
No direct evidence on willingness to pay was found. Contextual signals are strong: schools stage large, short graduation shows each year, and museums face boycott-limited programming and tight budgets (municipality 45%, ministry 5% or less at TAMA).

### Cited Findings
- About 400 Bezalel graduates in one show — [Maariv](https://www.maariv.co.il/culture/theater-art/Article-1027809)
- TAMA's funding mix and the boycott problem — [JPost](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/culture/article-902692)
- Ministry of Culture criteria changes threaten cooperative galleries — [Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.co.il/gallery/art/2026-05-18/ty-article/.premium/0000019e-3bb6-d222-a1be-7bb685590000)

### Inferences
- Schools and municipalities are plausible B2B buyers, through archive and documentation budgets or communications budgets. This should be validated in interviews, not assumed.

### Gaps
- Current documentation practices at Bezalel, Shenkar, Minshar and HaMidrasha (in-house photographers, Matterport use) were not found.
- No municipal tender or budget lines for exhibition documentation were found.
