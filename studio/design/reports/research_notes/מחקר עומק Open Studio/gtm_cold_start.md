# Go-to-market, cold start and marketing playbook for OPEN STUDIO (2026)

Method note: research done 2026-10-28 via web search. Direct page fetches were blocked in this session (datareportal.com, techcrunch.com, lennysnewsletter.com could not be resolved), so most findings come from search-result summaries of the cited pages; where a figure comes only from a secondary or vendor source this is flagged. "Evidence" = reported numbers or documented events; "Opinion" = frameworks, advice, analyst claims.

## 1. Cold-start theory: what the frameworks say and which cases fit OPEN STUDIO

### Takeaway
The strongest consensus (Chen, Rachitsky, marketplace practitioners) is: start with one tiny, dense "atomic network" (one city scene, one weekly cycle of openings), win the hard side (artists/galleries) first by solving a real problem for them, and use 1-3 acquisition channels only, mostly direct and offline. For OPEN STUDIO the atomic network is plausibly "Tel Aviv galleries + the people who go to Thursday/Friday openings".

### Cited Findings
- Opinion (framework): Chen's "atomic network" is the smallest network where enough people are present that everyone sticks around; density and interconnection matter more than size; examples: Bank of America launched the credit card only in Fresno (1958), Tinder and Facebook started in specific colleges; first atomic network is usually smaller than founders expect — [Indie Hackers summary of The Cold Start Problem](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-to-kickstart-a-marketplace-lessons-learned-from-andrew-chens-book-the-cold-start-problem-b0fdf81f6b); [Charter summary](https://www.charterworks.com/the-cold-start-problem-andrew-chen/)
- Opinion: The "hard side" (harder to acquire and retain, creates disproportionate value — e.g. Uber drivers) should be attracted first; sequence: seed supply, bring demand, keep focusing on supply; you must solve a crucial problem for the hard side, not just ship features — [Medium summary](https://medium.com/@samuelvandeth/the-cold-start-problem-6eff0ea52a84); [Andrew Chen, "Solve a hard problem"](https://andrewchen.com/solve-a-hard-problem-cold-start-problem/)
- Evidence-based cautionary case: Google+ had a huge launch but never formed atomic networks and failed — [Indie Hackers summary](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-to-kickstart-a-marketplace-lessons-learned-from-andrew-chens-book-the-cold-start-problem-b0fdf81f6b)
- Evidence (founder interviews compiled): Lenny Rachitsky found just 7 strategies explain the first 1,000 users of major consumer apps: go where target users gather offline, go where they gather online, invite friends, create FOMO, influencers, press, private communities. Most companies got early users from a single strategy; none from more than three. Direct outreach was most common; narrowly defining the target user comes first; tactics for the first 1,000 differ from the next 10,000 — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-the-biggest-consumer-apps-got); [The Next Web syndication](https://thenextweb.com/news/the-7-strategies-that-got-the-biggest-consumer-apps-their-first-1000-users-syndication)
- Opinion: NFX's James Currier is credited (secondary sources) with defining marketplace liquidity as the probability of selling what you list / finding what you want; practitioners advise measuring it by segment and geography and starting narrower than feels comfortable — [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/marketplace-growth/marketplace-liquidity); [Point Nine "WTF is marketplace liquidity"](https://writing.pointnine.com/p/wtf-is-marketplace-liquidity). (No primary NFX page retrieved.)

#### Case studies relevant to supply-first / invite / local
- Pinterest (evidence, secondary): began invite-only; first users were design bloggers Silbermann recruited; closed beta March 2010; Silbermann personally wrote to the first ~5,000 users and gave his phone number; only a few hundred users after the first quarter-year; invite requirement dropped in 2012 — [Product Habits](https://producthabits.com/how-pinterest-became-an-11-billion-company-by-organizing-the-worlds-hobbies/); [Innovators Uncensored](https://innovatorsuncensored.beehiiv.com/p/how-pinterest-got-its-first-users)
- Dribbble (evidence, CEO interview): bootstrapped ~a decade, remained invite-only, no traditional marketing; began with friends beta, then friends invite friends; invite system used to control growth — [Intercom podcast with Zack Onisko](https://www.intercom.com/blog/podcasts/dribbble-ceo-zack-onisko-community)
- Strava (secondary/blog sources, verify): founders sent 10 Garmin units to passionate cyclists, ran East vs West Coast competitions during the 2008 Tour de France, personally recruited local running/cycling clubs; cycling chosen because group rides are inherently social; clubs create denser network effects; adding running in 2012 tripled addressable market — [Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/strava-doing-things-that-dont-scale); [Tech Stadium Substack](https://techstadium.substack.com/p/strava-analyzing-the-growth-playbook); [Harvard D3](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-digit/submission/strava-striving-in-the-time-of-corona/)
- Partiful (evidence + analyst opinion): every invite is a link/landing page that travels through iMessage/Instagram/WhatsApp and guests RSVP without installing — the invite is the acquisition loop (Sacra, AI-generated analyst report); added 2M new users in 2025, ~500k monthly users Q1 2025, +400% YoY per Sensor Tower (NBC); a viral Timothée Chalamet lookalike contest started from a flyer with a QR + Partiful link; threats: Apple Invites (Feb 2025) and WhatsApp events (May 2024) — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/ca6c792b-7d6d-42a9-ae5f-0970a819c66c/); [NBC](https://www.nbcphiladelphia.com/news/business/money-report/meet-partiful-the-gen-z-party-planning-staple-thats-taking-on-apple/4164362/?amp=1); [NoGood (marketing blog, Mar 2026)](https://nogood.io/blog/partiful-marketing-strategy/)
- BeReal (evidence): 2022 surge spread via college campuses partly through a paid ambassador program ($30 per referral, $50 per download+review per Sacra; student papers reported similar); >10M DAU / 21.6M MAU late Aug 2022; later decline — Similarweb estimated 16.06M MAU, BeReal disputed and claimed 25M+ (Sept 2023); retention described as a known struggle — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BeReal); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/q/how-did-bereal-get-so-popular/); [TechCrunch Apr 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/22/bereal-hype-or-hit-what-to-know-about-the-gen-z-photo-sharing-app-climbing-the-charts/amp); [TechCrunch Sept 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/09/29/bereal-pushes-back-at-report-that-its-losing-steam-says-it-now-has-25m-daily-users)
- Locket (evidence): founder posted TikToks on a company account; users reused the original sound, one UK video got 5M+ views in a day; ~1.5M downloads early 2022 with no ad budget; raised $12.5M — [TechCrunch Jan 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/11/locket-an-app-for-sharing-photos-to-friends-homescreens-hits-the-top-of-the-app-store); [Appfigures](https://app.appfigures.com/resources/insights/20220114?f=5); [TechCrunch Aug 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/02/locket-app-that-lets-yor-post-photos-to-your-loved-ones-homescreens-raises-12-5m)
- Substack (company claims via secondary reporting): recommendations/network reportedly drive ~50% of new free subscriptions; Lenny reported 78% of his new free subs came from other newsletters' recommendations — [Substack "Turn on your growth engine"](https://on.substack.com/p/turn-on-your-growth-engine); [Pubstack Success](https://pubstacksuccess.substack.com/p/recommendations-a-substack-super)
- Luma (weak evidence): seed $3M Dec 2020 (Maven, Venrock), 500k+ users and 10k+ hosts by Dec 2020; job listing says "community is at the heart of Luma's growth" with a Creator Program; larger 2025 user figures only on unverified template sites — [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/?p=39471); [Built In job listing](https://builtin.com/job/community-marketer/4234246)

### Inferences
- OPEN STUDIO's hard side is artists/galleries (they must upload shows, clips, 3D tours). Like Pinterest/Dribbble, an invite-only artist side curated by the founder fits both the art world's prestige logic and Chen's "control density" advice.
- Partiful's mechanic maps directly: make each exhibition page a shareable link that opens without install (mobile web is an advantage here), so every gallery's opening invite on WhatsApp/Instagram becomes OPEN STUDIO acquisition.
- Strava's "clubs" analogue = existing art communities (studio buildings, artist collectives, art-school cohorts, gallery mailing lists).
- BeReal and Locket show viral spikes without a retention loop decay; the weekly openings cycle is OPEN STUDIO's natural retention loop.

### Gaps
- No primary-source detail retrieved for Behance, Meetup, Lemon8 (ByteDance paid creator seeding is widely reported but not verified here) or Partiful's very first users.
- NFX essays and Chen's book could not be fetched directly; all framework points are from summaries.

## 2. Cara (2024): why it exploded and what happened after

### Takeaway
Cara grew from ~40-50k to 650-700k users within about a week in June 2024 and passed 1M, triggered by artist anger at Meta's plan to train AI on public posts; growth was values-driven and organic, but it caused a large unplanned infrastructure bill, and there is no reliable public retention data — third-party web-traffic estimates suggest a much smaller active base by late 2025.

### Cited Findings
- Evidence: ~40k to ~650k users in about a week (June 2024), top of App Store; trigger was Meta training AI on public posts (opt-out only for EU users under GDPR); Cara refuses to host AI art until dataset ethics are resolved by regulation; later crossed 1M — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2790719); [Adam Levin blog, Oct 2024](https://adamlevin.com/2024/10/17/are-creatives-fleeing-instagram-due-to-ai/)
- Evidence: founder Zhang Jingna (photographer); user base sextupled over one weekend past 300k — [80.lv](https://origin.80.lv/articles/anti-ai-art-portfolio-platform-cara-reaches-300-000-users); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cara_(app))
- Weak evidence (secondary Chinese report, unverified): 300k to 900k in ~10 days with no paid ads; cloud bill ~$96k — [Baijing](https://www.baijing.cn/article/49078)
- Weak evidence (third-party estimate): cara.app ~948k web visits in Oct 2025, -11% vs Sept — [Semrush](https://vi.semrush.com/website/cara.app/overview)
- Opinion: 650k was ~0.03% of Instagram's user base — [The Strange Review](https://strangevc.beehiiv.com/p/can-cara-beat-instagram)

### Inferences
- Lesson for OPEN STUDIO: a clear, artist-protective stance (no AI-generated art, artist owns data, no AI training) is a powerful identity/PR hook in the art world; but a migration spike without a habit (weekly reason to return) and without the demand side (collectors/visitors) does not create a lasting network. Also: prepare infra cost ceilings before any viral moment.

### Gaps
- No reliable DAU/MAU or cohort retention for Cara after 2024.

## 3. Art-world launch precedents (Artsy, ArtRabbit, Tokyo Art Beat, Smartify, Bloomberg Connects, fairs, art schools, QR)

### Takeaway
Art platforms grew supply by being free (or near-free) for listings and by landing prestige anchors early (Artsy: Gagosian, Pace); listings platforms (ArtRabbit, Tokyo Art Beat) won through comprehensive, free, city-focused listings plus newsletters/maps; museum-guide apps grew through institutional partnerships. Fairs, gallery weekends and graduation shows concentrate tens of thousands of visitors into a few days — natural launch moments.

### Cited Findings
- Artsy (evidence): public launch Oct 2012 after ~2 years of testing; ~275 galleries + 50 institutions as partners by its first annual report; 1,500+ galleries by 2014 — [Artron 2014](https://m-news.artron.net/19700101/n561573.html); first charged galleries 1-6% commission on platform-generated sales (honor system), moved to subscriptions $400-1,400/month by mid-2013 — [Harvard D3 case](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-rctom/?p=7435); Gagosian and Pace among the first major galleries to join — [Freethink, "How Artsy became the Spotify of the fine art world"](https://www.freethink.com/?p=94233). Note: the claim that Artsy was "free first" for galleries is not confirmed by these sources (commission-only, i.e. no upfront fee, came first).
- ArtRabbit (evidence, own site): listing exhibitions/events is free worldwide; distribution via website, app, targeted emails and weekly city newsletters (London, UK, Berlin, LA, NY) and social; monetises via advertising/sponsorship and a paid Artist Monthly Plan; redesigned app has city guides, walking routes, maps, "save/track shows visited" — [ArtRabbit About](https://www.artrabbit.com/about/artrabbit); [ArtRabbit for Artists](https://www.artrabbit.com/what-you-can-do/artists); [Advertise](https://www.artrabbit.com/what-you-can-do/advertise)
- Tokyo Art Beat (evidence): launched late 2004 because there was little English info on Tokyo galleries; bilingual listings of 500+ galleries/museums; 18,000+ events listed by 2008; printed bimonthly Tokyo Art Map; ~200k visits/month; apps, NY and Kansai sister sites; NPO became a corporation in Oct 2020 with Startbahn partnership; later premium/membership model — [Art Space Tokyo interview](https://read.artspacetokyo.com/interviews/baron-fujitaka-thereaux/); [TAB 2008 Art Map](https://tokyoartbeat.com/en/articles/-/tokyo-art-map); [TAB x Startbahn 2021](https://tokyoartbeat.com/en/articles/-/tokyo-art-beat-joins-forces-with-startbahn); [AQ case study](https://aqworks.com/work/tokyo-art-beat)
- Smartify (evidence): launched Oct 2017 with 30 museums; later 1M users/85 venues; Jan 2025: 700+ partner organisations, 2M+ active users, usage +68% YoY, raised £1.5M; free for visitors, institutions pay SaaS + revenue share (not free for museums) — [UKTN Jan 2025](https://www.uktech.news/funding/shazam-for-art-museum-app-smartify-raises-1-5m-20250129); [Digital Catapult](https://www.digicatapult.org.uk/case-studies/study/smartify/)
- Bloomberg Connects (evidence): free for visitors and institutions (philanthropy-funded); 1,500+ museums/galleries/gardens per its site; DHM press release Dec 2025 says 1,200+ — [Bloomberg Connects](https://bloombergconnects.org/); [DHM Dec 2025](https://www.dhm.de/assets/DHM/Bilder/Pressebilder/2025/251201_Free_Museum_Guide_with_Bloomberg_Connects_DHM.pdf)
- Freshpaint Tel Aviv (organizer claim): annual since 2008, 40,000+ visitors/year (older sources: 30,000+ over six days); 2026 (17th edition) ran June 24-29 at Kremenetski Technical Center with 60+ exhibitions — [Freshpaint Group](https://freshpaint.co.il/en/freshpaint-group-en); [Time Out Israel](https://www.timeout.com/israel/news/everything-you-need-to-know-about-fresh-paint-israels-major-art-and-design-fair-021218); [LetsDataScience news on 2026](https://letsdatascience.com/news/freshpaint-2026-highlights-hands-on-art-amid-ai-rise-16fa1a27)
- Gallery Weekend Berlin (evidence): invitation-only, max 55 Berlin galleries; 2025 edition May 2-4 with ~51-52 galleries, 80+ artistic positions, ~59 locations; "over 20,000 people" is an undated secondary claim — [GWB About](https://gallery-weekend-berlin.de/about); [Capitain Petzel](https://www.capitainpetzel.de/news/494-gallery-weekend-berlin-may-1-4-2025); [Singulart](https://www.singulart.com/blog/en/2025/03/25/experience-berlin-the-annual-gallery-weekend-berlin-review-sfa-af/)
- Bezalel graduate exhibition (evidence): 500+ graduates in 2026 (≈400 in 2024), July 24-Aug 6 2026, Jerusalem, open to 22:00; Bezalel describes it as drawing tens of thousands of visitors — [Bezalel 2026 event](https://bezalel.ac.il/en/events/664929); [Bezalel graduates website 2026](https://bezalel.ac.il/en/news/664961); [Bezalel galleries & graduate exhibitions](https://bezalel.ac.il/en/Gallery_Graduates_Undergraduates)
- QR in exhibitions (evidence, institutional self-reports): National Museum of Scotland — Galloway Hoard: ~26k scans vs ~65k visitors; Typewriter Revolution: 6k scans vs 132k visits — [NMS blog](https://blog.nms.ac.uk/?p=30536); Brooklyn Museum: 1.77% of visitors scanned entrance tags, codes on objects ~0.059% of visitors — [Brooklyn Museum tech blog](https://techblog.brooklynmuseum.org/tag/qrcode/feed/index.html); Cambridge museums: QR got 9x more page views than NFC — [Cambridge Museums blog 2022](https://www.museums.cam.ac.uk/blog/2022/01/06/to-qr-or-nfc-that-is-the-question/)

### Inferences
- Supply offer for galleries should be free at launch (like ArtRabbit/Bloomberg Connects) and make the gallery look good to its own audience (3D tour, studio clips = something they can't easily make themselves), with 2-3 prestige anchor galleries in Tel Aviv recruited personally (Artsy's Gagosian/Pace analogue).
- QR scan rates range from <1% to ~40% of visitors depending on placement and value of what's behind it: one strong QR at the entrance/wall text offering something real (the 3D tour, the artist's studio clip, "follow this artist") beats QR on every label.
- Launch calendar: Freshpaint (June), Bezalel/Shenkar/HaMidrasha graduation shows (July), plus recurring Tel Aviv opening nights are concentrated moments to seed both sides at once.
- Tokyo Art Beat shows a comprehensive bilingual city listings + printed map + newsletter could sustain ~200k visits/month in one city — a realistic ceiling reference for a Tel Aviv-first product.

### Gaps
- No source confirming Artsy was free for galleries at launch; no ArtRabbit growth numbers; no Shenkar/HaMidrasha visitor figures; no Fresh Paint 2025/2026 official attendance.

## 4. Marketing channels for 2026 (short video, creators, newsletters, WhatsApp, PR, SEO/ASO, referral, Product Hunt)

### Takeaway
For a no-budget art app, the evidence favours: founder-led short video (Locket-style), shareable no-install links into WhatsApp/Instagram (Partiful-style), a city newsletter (ArtRabbit/Tokyo Art Beat-style) and recommendation/referral loops (Substack-style). Paid acquisition early is warned against by practitioners. Product Hunt has little documented relevance for a local, non-tech art audience.

### Cited Findings
- Evidence (academic): museum TikTok research — 2024 study of 313 videos from 7 top museum accounts found varied formats; humorous and topical content engages more but not all museums use it; 2025 study of 9,223 comments found predominantly positive sentiment and creators' key role in museum visibility — [UCLan 2025 paper](https://clok.uclan.ac.uk/55289); [Heriot-Watt research note](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/research-note-sensational-museums-on-tiktok-reaching-young-virtual-tourists-with-short-videos/); [Polish study, Zarządzanie Mediami](https://ejournals.eu/en/journal/zarzadzanie-mediami/article/nieformalnie-i-w-nawiazaniu-do-biezacych-wydarzen-komunikacja-muzeow-na-tiktoku)
- Evidence: Locket's TikTok virality from a founder account (see section 1) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/11/locket-an-app-for-sharing-photos-to-friends-homescreens-hits-the-top-of-the-app-store)
- Evidence/claim: Substack network recommendations ~50% of new free subs — [Substack](https://on.substack.com/p/turn-on-your-growth-engine)
- Opinion: Andrew Chen warns paid marketing tends to hit a "local max" as primary channel; track CAC per channel (not blended); fix churn and frequency first — [andrewchen.com, Paid marketing](https://andrewchen.com/paid-marketing-addiction/); growth is getting more expensive because of platform consolidation — [andrewchen.com](https://andrewchen.com/hello-2018-books-essays-and-more-from-the-past-year/)
- Evidence: ArtRabbit uses weekly city newsletters as a core distribution channel — [ArtRabbit About](https://www.artrabbit.com/about/artrabbit)

### Inferences
- Content engine: studio clips are already the product's content; repost them as Reels/TikTok with "see the full show in 3D" link. Humor/topical formats and artist-faces perform better than polished institutional posts (per museum TikTok studies).
- Referral loop: "artist invites their collectors/followers" (artist brings demand) is the strongest built-in loop for a supply-first network; every follow/share link should open in the browser without sign-up.
- SEO: Hebrew + English pages per exhibition ("תערוכות השבוע בתל אביב" / "exhibitions this week Tel Aviv") are cheap long-term demand capture since the search showed no strong current listings player in results (inference from a single search, see gaps).

### Gaps
- No hard 2025-2026 data on #arttok performance, ASO for art apps, Product Hunt outcomes for consumer/cultural apps, or SEO volume for "exhibitions Tel Aviv" queries (would need keyword tools).

## 5. Israeli specifics: platforms and how art audiences find openings

### Takeaway
Israel is near-saturated on WhatsApp, Facebook and YouTube (90%+ usage in surveys), with Instagram at ~55-70%; social media identities ~7.0M (73% of population). For art audiences, discovery runs through Facebook events, Instagram, gallery mailing lists and WhatsApp groups, but no systematic data was found. Erev Rav is an art-criticism journal, not a listings newsletter.

### Cited Findings
- Evidence: DataReportal Digital 2026 Israel — 7.01M social media user identities (Oct 2025) = 73.4% of population; 8.72M internet users end-2025 — [DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-israel) (via search summary; page fetch was blocked)
- Evidence (ad-reach estimates): Facebook 7.59M users (79.2% of population), Instagram 5.33M (55.6%), Messenger 5.22M, Dec 2025 — [NapoleonCat](https://stats.napoleoncat.com/social-media-users-in-israel/2025/)
- Evidence (survey, early 2023): over 90% use YouTube, WhatsApp, Facebook; Instagram ~70% — [Israel Internet Association via Internet Society](https://news.internetsociety.org/israel-internet-association-unveils-an-index-of-internet-use-in-israel/); a separate Israeli report puts WhatsApp at 99% usage — [Calcalist Tech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/rkqhuhexfl)
- Evidence: Erev Rav is an independent journal of Arts, Culture and Society (est. 2010, eds. Yonatan Amir, Ronen Eidelman), publishing essays/reviews, printed editions and events; no openings calendar found — [Erev Rav English archive](https://erev-rav.com/archives/english)
- Evidence: municipal venue search exists (visit.tel-aviv.gov.il) and Time Out Israel covers art listings — [Tel Aviv venues](https://visit.tel-aviv.gov.il/Pages/SearchVenues.aspx); [Time Out Israel](https://www.timeout.com/israel/art/litvak-contemporary)

### Inferences
- WhatsApp is the distribution layer: exhibition pages must render a rich link preview (OG image/title in Hebrew + English) and be one tap from WhatsApp; a weekly "openings this week" WhatsApp channel/community post from OPEN STUDIO is the Israeli equivalent of ArtRabbit's newsletter.
- Facebook (79% reach) still matters for 30+ collectors; Instagram for artists and younger visitors.
- Hebrew-first UI text for the Israeli demand side, with English for artists' international audience, is suggested by Tokyo Art Beat's bilingual model (inference).

### Gaps
- No 2025 Bezeq Internet Report figures found; no survey on how Israeli art audiences discover openings (Facebook events vs Instagram vs WhatsApp); no data on Israeli art newsletters' reach. A founder-run survey at openings would fill this.

## 6. Launch metrics: activation, north star, retention, PMF, liquidity

### Takeaway
Use a weekly-habit north star (e.g., weekly active visitors who open an exhibition/tour or follow an artist), Lenny's consumer-social retention benchmark (good ≈25%, great ≈45% of users active at 6 months), the Sean Ellis 40% "very disappointed" survey, and a liquidity metric per side (share of Tel Aviv openings listed; share of visitor sessions that find a relevant current show).

### Cited Findings
- Evidence (practitioner survey): consumer social retention at 6 months — good ~25%, great ~45% (Chang >40%, Winters >45%, Rothenberg >50%); not required for PMF — [Lenny's Newsletter "What is good retention"](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-good-retention-issue-29); [Casey Winters](https://www.caseyaccidental.com/p/what-is-good-retention-an-exhaustive-benchmark-study-with-lenny-rachitsky)
- Opinion/heuristic: Sean Ellis test — ≥40% of active users "very disappointed" if product disappeared signals PMF (from 100+ startups); pair with a flattening retention curve; Superhuman reportedly went from 22% to 58% — [Swetrix](https://swetrix.com/blog/how-to-measure-product-market-fit); [St-Hakky](https://book.st-hakky.com/en/business/pmf-measuring-methods-40-percent-rule)
- Opinion: liquidity is a probability (match/fill rate), tracked per segment and geography; a vendor guide cites >60% match rate as strong, not NFX data — [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/marketplace-growth/marketplace-liquidity); [Causo (vendor)](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/marketplace-liquidity-seed)
- Opinion: Chen — fix churn and frequency before scaling paid — [andrewchen.com](https://andrewchen.com/paid-marketing-addiction/)

### Inferences
- Suggested OPEN STUDIO metric set: Supply liquidity = % of the week's Tel Aviv openings present in the app (target: majority of a defined gallery list); Activation (visitor) = follows ≥1 artist/gallery and opens ≥1 show within first session; Activation (artist) = profile + current show + ≥1 clip; North star = weekly visitors who view a current show (tour or page); retention cohorts by week (opening cycle is weekly), measured at W1/W4/M6; Ellis survey once ~100 weekly actives exist.

### Gaps
- No art-app-specific retention benchmarks found (Artsy/Smartify do not publish cohorts).

## 7. Pitfalls: launching too broad, paid too early, no retention loop

### Takeaway
Documented failures cluster around big launches without dense networks (Google+), virality without habit (BeReal's decline), and spikes driven by external triggers that fade (Cara); practitioner opinion warns against paid acquisition as primary channel before retention is fixed.

### Cited Findings
- Evidence: Google+ big launch, no atomic networks — [Indie Hackers summary of Chen](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-to-kickstart-a-marketplace-lessons-learned-from-andrew-chens-book-the-cold-start-problem-b0fdf81f6b)
- Evidence: BeReal MAU decline from 2022 peak, retention struggles — [TechCrunch Sept 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/09/29/bereal-pushes-back-at-report-that-its-losing-steam-says-it-now-has-25m-daily-users); [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/report/bereal)
- Opinion: marketplaces that achieve durable liquidity started in a smaller space than felt comfortable — [Raftlabs](https://www.raftlabs.com/blog/two-sided-marketplace-failure-rate)
- Evidence: Partiful faces platform-native competition (Apple Invites, WhatsApp events) — [NBC](https://www.nbcphiladelphia.com/news/business/money-report/meet-partiful-the-gen-z-party-planning-staple-thats-taking-on-apple/4164362/?amp=1)
- Evidence: Cara's surge created a large unplanned infra bill (unverified figure ~$96k) — [Baijing](https://www.baijing.cn/article/49078)

### Inferences
- Specific risks for OPEN STUDIO: (1) going national/international before Tel Aviv has a majority of openings listed; (2) a demand side with nothing new each week (stale listings kill return visits); (3) dependence on Instagram/WhatsApp, which could ship similar features (Partiful's lesson); (4) 3D tours are expensive per exhibition — supply onboarding must stay low-effort for galleries or the hard side stalls.
- Suggested sequencing (synthesis): Phase 0 (4-8 wks) — founder personally onboards 20-30 artists + 5-10 Tel Aviv galleries incl. 2-3 prestige anchors, free; invite-only artist side. Phase 1 — weekly "openings this week" via WhatsApp/Instagram + shareable exhibition links + one QR at each participating show; founder-led short video from studio clips. Phase 2 — tentpole moment (Freshpaint in June, graduation shows in July) with full coverage of the event. Phase 3 — artist-invites-collectors referral loop; Ellis survey; expand to Jerusalem/Haifa only once Tel Aviv liquidity holds.

### Gaps
- No documented failure post-mortems of art-specific discovery apps found in this round (e.g., closed gallery-listing apps); worth a separate search.
