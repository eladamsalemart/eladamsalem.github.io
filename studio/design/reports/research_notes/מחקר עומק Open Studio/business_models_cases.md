# Business models, pricing, unit economics and case studies: art-tech and two-sided creative platforms

Research date: 2026-10-28. Method: web search (about 20 queries). Several primary sites (theartnewspaper.com, lennysnewsletter.com, placera.se) could not be fetched from this session, so some figures rest on search-result summaries of those pages; this is marked where it matters. "Verified" = stated by the company or its filings or press quoting them; "estimate" = analyst or aggregator figure; "anecdotal" = forum or review.

## 1. Incumbent art-tech models: Artsy, Artnet, Ocula, Artland, Artlogic, Artwork Archive, Saatchi Art, Singulart

### Takeaway
The model that has kept art platforms going is **galleries pay a monthly subscription** (Artsy, Ocula, Artland), with commission added on top. Even the market leaders are under strain. Artsy ran layoffs in 2019 and 2023, could not reach profitability in 2023, and in 2026 was merged with a shrinking Artnet under private equity, followed by more layoffs. Gallery SaaS (Artlogic, Artwork Archive) is the steadier, priced-per-seat business. Pure-commission artist marketplaces take 40–50%.

### Cited Findings
**Artsy**
- 2020: Artsy proposed raising one gallery's monthly fee from **$375 to $650 (+40%+)** and adding a **15% commission** on "buy now" sales. This caused visible frustration among smaller dealers — [The Art Newspaper, "Smaller dealers are frustrated by digital sales platforms" (2020)](https://theartnewspaper.com/news/smaller-dealers-are-frustrated-by-digital-sales-platforms) (via search summary; page not fetchable here).
- Current gallery plans are not public ("schedule a demo… learn which subscription plan is right for you") — [Artsy partners page](https://partners.artsy.net/gallery-growth-tools).
- Model: monthly gallery subscriptions plus commission on some marketplace and auction sales — [Wikipedia: Artsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artsy_(website)).
- Layoffs: about 20 staff (~10%) in 2019 under new CEO Mike Steib — [The Art Newspaper 2019](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2019/09/30/artsy-lays-off-around-20-employees-under-new-leadership). About 35 staff (~15%) in mid-2023. Steib said revenue was growing, but a weak art market had put profitability out of reach that year — [FASHIONSNAP citing ARTnews, 2023](https://www.fashionsnap.com/article/2023-07-05/artsy-layoff/); [TheCollector](https://www.thecollector.com/artsy-decided-to-dismiss-35-employees/).
- Funding (estimate): **$135M over 10 rounds**, including a reported Series E of $32.8M on 6 May 2025 — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/artsy/financials). Other trackers say $126M — [Multiples.vc](https://multiples.vc/private-comps/artsy). Last known valuation **$275M (2017)** — [Grape Collective citing PitchBook](https://grapecollective.com/articles/100-million-counting-). Partner count is quoted as "3,000+" or "4,000+" galleries and auction houses (aggregators, inconsistent). **No public revenue figure was found.**
- CFO Jeffrey Yin became CEO in June 2024. In April 2026, Beowolff Capital (PE, Andrew Wolff) combined Artsy and Artnet. Artnet was taken private, and dozens were laid off at both companies a day after the deal; Artnet's Berlin/German entity was closed — [Hyperallergic 2026](https://hyperallergic.com/art-movements-dozens-laid-off-at-artnet-and-artsy/); [Artforum](https://www.artforum.com/news/bloodbath-follows-artsy-artnet-consolidation-1234748056/); [Artlyst: "two struggling companies"](https://artlyst.com/artsy-and-artnet-can-two-struggling-companies-spin-art-into-gold/).

**Artnet AG (was public until 2026)**
- FY2024 revenue **about 22.2M** (sources disagree on USD or EUR), with **3.5M in cost savings** from restructuring. Media revenue fell 3.7% and marketplace revenue 3.4% — [Artnet 2024 annual report release, Aug 2025 (Placera)](https://www.placera.se/pressmeddelanden/artnet-ag-reports-resilience-and-strategic-innovation-in-2024-amidst-market-headwinds-20250805); [Simply Wall St](https://simplywall.st/stock/db/art) (earnings −€1.4M for FY2024, estimate; it may be EBIT).
- Q3 2024 alone had EBIT of −€1.4M, and the FY2024 forecast was cut — [Artnet Q3 2024 prelim](https://app.boersengefluester.de/en/newswire/DE000A1K0375/artnet-ag/artnet-publishes-preliminary-figures-for-the-third-quarter-of-2024-and-adjusts-forecast-for-the-financial-year-2024-2025337).
- H1 2025 revenue fell **~12%** (Puck: −11.4%), mainly from media; H1 media revenue was $3.31M — [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/art-movements-dozens-laid-off-at-artnet-and-artsy/); [Puck](https://puck.news/newsletter_content/drahis-motivations-artnets-new-era-koons-news/).

**Ocula**
- 2017: a "competitive monthly fee of **$125**" for galleries. Membership is by application, with a gallerist selection committee and about 160 galleries — [e-flux call for applications (2017)](https://www.e-flux.com/announcements/184577/call-for-applications). In 2020 it launched Online Viewing Rooms that show prices and gallery contacts so collectors buy direct — [Ocula](https://ocula.com/magazine/art-news/ocula-launches-online-viewing-rooms/). No commission was disclosed.

**Artland (closest analogue: 3D exhibition recordings)**
- Galleries pay a monthly subscription, and **billing starts only after the gallery's first sale**. It is free for collectors and the public — [TechCrunch 2017](https://techcrunch.com/2017/11/17/artland-is-a-social-art-market-that-connects-galleries-and-buyers/?ncid=rss). $1M seed in 2018 — [TechCrunch 2018](https://techcrunch.com/2018/03/13/artland-closes-1m-seed-for-its-social-art-marketplace/?ncid=rss).
- 2019: 3D archiving of exhibitions that galleries can embed on their own site and newsletter; 50,000+ users, 250+ galleries — [Artfix Daily press release](https://artfixdaily.com/artwire/release/121-artland-with-its-3d-online-art-galleries-enters-new-york-in-indepe). In 2020 it powered the virtual edition of the Untitled art fair — [Untitled Art](https://untitledartfairs.com/news/post/untitled-art-and-artland-partner-together-to-present-the-worlds-first-virtual-reality-art-fair).
- A starting price of "$69/month" is listed by a comparison site; unverified — [PictureIt](https://www.pictureit.co/blog/best-virtual-art-gallery-software).

**Gallery and artist SaaS**
- Artlogic (gallery CRM/database) is priced per user: Gallery Management Professional **£90/user/month** (annual), Expert **£132/user/month** — [Artlogic support](https://support.artlogic.net/hc/en-gb/articles/14715213432988). Bundles (website plus database) run about £156–£311/month according to aggregators — [Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/170623/Artlogic/pricing/); [Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/omni/instead-of/artlogic/pricing).
- Artwork Archive (artist and collector inventory): artist and collector plans from **~$6–8/month**; organisations $24–29 / $59 / $139 per month; 14-day trial — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/169087/Artwork-Archive/); [G2](https://g2.com/products/artwork-archive/pricing). No customer count found.

**Artist marketplaces (commission)**
- Saatchi Art: **40%** commission on originals according to 2026 guides — [PriceYourPainting 2026](https://priceyourpainting.substack.com/p/where-to-sell-your-paintings-in-2026); [Foundmyself](https://www.foundmyself.com/blog/where-to-sell-artwork-online/). Older sources say 35% — [Harvard D3](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-digit/submission/saatchi-art-equal-opportunity-to-sell-and-own-original-art/index.html). Not verified against Saatchi's own terms.
- Singulart: **~50%** commission plus paid artist tiers (Silver/Gold/Platinum) — [Foundmyself](https://www.foundmyself.com/blog/where-to-sell-artwork-online/). Unverified with the company.

### Inferences
- OPEN STUDIO's ₪390/month gallery plan (~$105) sits between Ocula's 2017 $125 and Artsy's 2020 $375–650. That is plausible for small and mid Israeli galleries, but Artsy's history shows galleries resist price rises unless the platform can point to sales.
- Artland's "pay only after first sale" is a direct precedent for lowering gallery risk. For OPEN STUDIO, the analogue is "first tour free" or "pay only if X visits".
- Artlogic's model (per-seat, sticky back-office) earns more reliably than "exposure" subscriptions. A documentation and archive service, meaning a 3D tour as a permanent exhibition record, is closer to Artlogic's value (a need) than to Artsy's (a hoped-for lead).

### Gaps
- Artsy's revenue, profitability and current gallery price (2023–2026) are not public; no reliable source was found.
- Artnet's exact FY2024 net result and currency could not be confirmed (the report PDF was not fetched).
- Ocula's current fee, Artland's current price and Artwork Archive's customer count were not found.

## 2. Post-mortems: Paddle8, Artfinder, Vortic, Exhibbit, Artspace, VR galleries after COVID

### Takeaway
Documented failures came from **finance and governance** (Paddle8), from **chronic downsizing after acquisition** (Artspace), or quietly **faded after the COVID bump** (Vortic and VR fairs). No clean public post-mortem with CAC or churn figures was found for any of them. The repeated pattern: galleries pay for online tools in crises (2020) and stop when physical openings and fairs return.

### Cited Findings
- **Paddle8**: Chapter 11 filing in March 2020. Court proceedings showed it used money owed to consignors, mostly charity auction proceeds, to run operations. Before that came the 2016 merger with Auctionata (insolvent in 2017) and sales to Native (2018) and FaceBank (2019, with a $10M loan). The CEO and board resigned, and the company was liquidated — [Center for Art Law, "A melting ice cube" (2023)](https://itsartlaw.org/2023/06/30/a-melting-ice-cube-the-bankruptcy-of-paddle8); [The Art Newspaper 2020](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/online-auction-house-paddle8-files-for-bankruptcy); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddle8).
- **Artspace**: acquired by Phaidon in 2014 — [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industrynews/publisher-news/article/63666-phaidon-acquires-artspace.html). In 2017 all but 5 of its 19 employees were let go — [ARTnews](https://www.artnews.com/art-news/market/artspace-offs-nearly-all-employees-roiling-online-art-market-landscape-7565). Artnet called its sale a sign of an "inevitable shake-out in online art sales" — [Artnet News](https://news.artnet.com/market/artspace-sale-augurs-inevitable-shake-out-in-online-art-sales-78371?amp=1). It still operates (2026); there are anecdotal Trustpilot complaints about its fairs.
- **Artfinder** (UK, founded 2010): about €7M raised over several rounds, the last a 2020 crowdfund — [funding.tech.eu](https://funding.tech.eu/companies/20C8FF2A-21FD-44C6-8E4A-E1F2EC7E1C5F); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/53962-84). Revenue is estimated at about $8M (ZoomInfo, unverified). New artist applications were paused, to reopen 1 Dec 2025 — [Artfinder sell page](https://artfinder.com/sell). 2026 Trustpilot reviews show artists complaining about policy changes ("made us raise our prices") — [Trustpilot](https://br.trustpilot.com/review/artfinder.com). **No evidence of insolvency found.**
- **Vortic** (London, 2020): VR/AR virtual gallery spaces sold as a subscription ("Vortic Curate"). It hosted The London Collective (40 galleries, Feb–Apr 2021) and theVOV historic-exhibitions programme in 2021 — [Artnet News](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/new-extended-reality-app-1812807); [Museums Association 2021](https://www.museumsassociation.org/museums-journal/news/2021/04/landmark-exhibitions-of-the-past-resurrected-on-xr-platform/); [digitalmeetsculture](https://www.digitalmeetsculture.net/article/vortic-the-new-virtual-reality-app-brings-art-galleries-straight-to-collectors-homes/). No coverage after 2021 was found.
- **Online art post-COVID**: online art sales were $10.8B in 2022 (+6%, slowing growth). Online-only sales at Christie's, Sotheby's and Phillips fell ~35% to $898M. The share of collectors buying online dropped from 85% to 78% — [Hiscox Online Art Trade Report 2023](https://www.hiscox.co.uk/online-art-trade-report/). Hiscox ended the series after 10 years, and ArtTactic continues it alone — [ArtTactic](https://arttactic.com/reports/online-art-market-report-2024).

### Inferences
- The Vortic/VR pattern is the biggest risk for 3D tours: they are valued as a substitute for visiting during a crisis, not as a complement. OPEN STUDIO should position the tour as a **record, archive, and a way to reach people who missed the show**, with low upfront cost, not as "virtual visiting".
- The Paddle8 lesson for a commission or ticketing model: never hold consignor or customer funds in operating cash. Use escrow or a payment provider with split payouts.

### Gaps
- No founder post-mortem with CAC, churn or gallery willingness-to-pay figures was found for any of these companies.
- No information was found on Exhibbit. Vortic's current status is unknown.

## 3. Analogous creator platforms: Patreon, Substack, Bandcamp, Linktree, Spotify for Artists, Behance

### Takeaway
Creator platforms that last take **8–15% of creator revenue** (Patreon, Substack, Bandcamp) or charge **low flat subscriptions ($5–35/month)** for tools (Linktree, Artwork Archive). Free-to-paid conversion benchmarks sit around **3–8% for self-serve freemium**. OPEN STUDIO's ₪29/month (~$8) Studio Pro is priced in line with Linktree Starter or Pro and Artwork Archive's artist tier.

### Cited Findings
- **Patreon**: revenue about **$140M in 2024**, an estimated $179M in 2025. Blended take rate rose from ~7% (2020) to ~8.5%; most creators are on the 8% plan — [Sacra (estimate)](https://sacra.com/research/patreon). Fees are 8–12% plus payment processing — [helloaudio.fm](https://helloaudio.fm/patreon-vs-substack/).
- **Substack**: 10% of paid subscription revenue plus Stripe fees. "5M+ paid subscriptions" (early 2025). Paid subscriptions are ~5.7% of active subscriptions on a 35M base (Sept 2025); other sources put the total at 50M. Conflicting, all estimates — [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/substack-users); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/patreon/).
- **Bandcamp**: **15% on digital, falling to 10% after $5,000 in sales; 10% on physical**, plus ~4–6% processing. On average 82% of money goes to the artist or label — [Bandcamp help](https://get.bandcamp.help/hc/en-us/articles/23020665520663-What-are-Bandcamp-s-fees); [Bandcamp 2010 launch post](https://blog.bandcamp.com/2010/07/14/its-a-business-model/). Streaming platforms take about 30% by comparison — [Alderbrook (commentary)](https://alderbrook.substack.com/p/why-epic-games-bought-bandcamp).
- **Linktree**: 2025 tiers were Starter $5, Pro $9, Premium $24 per month. After a November 2025 rise they are Starter **$8**, Pro **$15**, Premium **$35** (annual billing ~20% cheaper). Below Premium, a transaction fee applies on commerce — [u2l.ai 2026](https://u2l.ai/blog/linktree-pricing); [Replug](https://replug.io/blog/linktree-pricing).
- **Freemium conversion benchmarks**: Lenny Rachitsky and Kyle Poyar surveyed 1,000+ products (Aug 2023), defining conversion as the share of new accounts paying within 6 months. As reported secondhand, self-serve freemium converts at **~6–8%** (good–great) and sales-assisted at 10–15% — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-a-good-free-to-paid-conversion) (paywalled); [Userpilot summary](https://userpilot.com/blog/freemium-to-premium). A ChartMogul/ProductLed median is ~8% across 200 products, while First Page Sage reports an average of 3.7% for traditional freemium — [marketingschool.io](https://marketingschool.io/what-is-a-good-free-to-paid-conversion-rate-with-data-2553).

### Inferences
- For a planning base case, use **2–4% of active artists converting to ₪29 Pro**, not 6–8%. The B2B SaaS benchmarks assume a product with clear work value; artists have low incomes and many free alternatives (Instagram, Linktree free).
- The Bandcamp and Spotify for Artists lesson: give artists their core analytics and profile **free**, and charge for things tied to money (private collector links, sales tools) or a take on sales. A paywall on vanity stats converts poorly.
- Benchmark: 1,000 active artists × 3% × ₪29 ≈ ₪870/month. Artist subscriptions will not carry the business early on; galleries and institutions must.

### Gaps
- No artist-specific churn data was found for Patreon or Linktree. Behance Pro, Adobe Portfolio and Squarespace prices were not retrieved. No Spotify for Artists disclosure was retrieved in this pass. Patreon's count of paying patrons was not found.

## 4. Gallery willingness to pay and documentation costs

### Takeaway
Galleries spend heavily on **fairs (~1/3 of total costs)**, payroll and rent. A third of dealer sales are now online, mostly through the gallery's own website, email and social media, not third-party platforms. A 3D scan service costs roughly **$0.08–0.20 per sq ft, or ~$150–350 per small space**, in older US market data. A ₪590 per-exhibition tour (~$160) is at the low end of that range.

### Cited Findings
- Art Basel & UBS report (latest edition, 2025 data, reported 2026): dealer costs rose ~5%. For primary-market galleries, payroll and rent are each 21% of costs. Fair booths are 15% plus 17% in related costs, so **fairs are ~32% of spending**. 29% of dealers are cutting back on fairs (31% in 2024, 19% in 2023). 38% were less profitable — [Artnet News](https://news.artnet.com/market/art-dealer-sales-2025-2754173).
- 2025 report (2024 data): **online channels were 33% of dealer sales (37% including fair OVRs)**, mostly through dealers' own sites, email and social — [Hyperallergic/Artlogic](https://hyperallergic.com/what-2025-art-basel-ubs-report-means-dealers-artlogic-breaks-it-down). 83% of galleries cite high participation costs as the main challenge at fairs (First Thursday survey 2025) — [ARTnews](https://www.artnews.com/art-news/market/new-2025-survey-galleries-art-fair-model-first-thursday-1234749459).
- Matterport service providers: prevailing retail rate **$0.08–0.12/sq ft**; Matterport reps cited $0.15–0.20. Small spaces of ~1,000 sq ft cost **$150–200+**. Example: $350 for 4,696 sq ft. Extras such as tags, guided tours and floor plans are charged on top. Competition can push rates down to $0.03. **Anecdotal, mostly 2016–2019** — [WGAN forum](https://forum.we-get-around.com/topic/7444/matterport-charge-per-square-foot/); [WGAN retail pricing](https://www.wegetaroundnetwork.com/topic/441/retail-pricing/); [WGAN](https://forum.we-get-around.com/topic/20/what-does-someone-charge-for-this-service/).

### Inferences
- The strongest pitch to a gallery replaces something it already pays for: exhibition documentation photography plus a website/newsletter asset plus reaching collectors who could not visit. It should not add a new "platform listing" line.
- Fair costs make up a third of dealer spending, and dealers are cutting back. A digital presence that partly replaces fair exposure is a budget argument. The claim must be backed by the visit and lead analytics the tour produces.
- ₪590 per exhibition is roughly the price of a basic Matterport scan, but OPEN STUDIO also includes hosting and distribution. ₪390/month only makes sense for galleries running about one show a month or more; most Israeli galleries run 6–10 shows a year, so per-exhibition pricing or an annual package of N tours fits better (inference).

### Gaps
- No Israeli figures were found for gallery marketing budgets or the cost of exhibition photography (₪ per shoot). This needs local interviews. No recent (2024–2026) Matterport rate card was found.

## 5. Pricing research methods

### Takeaway
At OPEN STUDIO's sample sizes (tens of galleries), Van Westendorp is only directional. Pair it with **paid pilots and pre-sales** (a concierge MVP) and Gabor-Granger offers at fixed prices.

### Cited Findings
- Van Westendorp PSM asks four open price questions and produces an acceptable range plus Indifference and Optimal price points. It does not give a demand curve without the Newton-Miller-Smith extension — [Solvimon](https://www.solvimon.com/glossary/van-westendorp).
- Suggested sample sizes: PSM 30–60 per segment; Gabor-Granger 50–100; qualitative pricing interviews 8–15 per segment. PSM assumes respondents understand the product — [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/willingness-to-pay-research). It is good for new products without a direct reference and combines well with Gabor-Granger — [Intotheminds](https://www.intotheminds.com/blog/en/van-westendorp-pricing-study/).

### Inferences
- Israel has perhaps 100–200 commercial galleries (verify in the market research), so PSM at n≥30 per segment is barely feasible. The practical method: 10–15 structured interviews, then 3–5 paid concierge tours at different prices (₪390 / ₪590 / ₪890). Count how many say yes and pay. A signed or paid commitment is worth more than a survey answer.

### Gaps
- No art-sector-specific pricing study was found.

## 6. Marketplace dynamics: take rates, liquidity, disintermediation

### Takeaway
Art marketplaces with commission sit at 15% (Artsy "buy now", 2020) up to 40–50% (Saatchi, Singulart). Disintermediation is built in: Ocula's viewing rooms deliberately send buyers directly to the gallery, and most online dealer sales happen on the dealers' own channels.

### Cited Findings
- Artsy added a 15% commission on top of the subscription (2020) — [The Art Newspaper](https://theartnewspaper.com/news/smaller-dealers-are-frustrated-by-digital-sales-platforms).
- Ocula OVRs show prices and gallery contacts so purchases go direct — [Ocula](https://ocula.com/magazine/art-news/ocula-launches-online-viewing-rooms/).
- Most dealers' online sales are on their own platforms — [Hyperallergic/Artlogic](https://hyperallergic.com/what-2025-art-basel-ubs-report-means-dealers-artlogic-breaks-it-down).
- Saatchi takes 40% and Singulart ~50%; Bandcamp 10–15%; Patreon ~8.5%; Substack 10% (sources above).

### Inferences
- For high-priced unique works where the gallery has a personal relationship with collectors, a transaction commission will leak, because buyers and galleries move off-platform. Commission is enforceable only where the platform runs checkout (prints, editions, low-priced works) or for artists without a gallery (the Saatchi/Singulart model). OPEN STUDIO should start with subscriptions or fees and keep commission optional, for example on prints and editions or a low-rate "buy now".

### Gaps
- An a16z marketplace take-rate guide was not retrieved in this pass. Liquidity metrics for art marketplaces were not found.

## 7. Unit-economics benchmarks for SMB SaaS

### Takeaway
SMB SaaS typically loses **3–5% of customers a month (31–46% a year)**. CAC payback runs **~9–15 months**, and median LTV:CAC is **~2–3:1**. These are aggregator figures; treat them as ranges.

### Cited Findings
- SMB monthly churn of 3–5% (31–46% a year), from Optifai's sample of 939 companies, via an aggregator — [Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/smb-customer-lifetime-value-benchmarks-2026).
- Median LTV:CAC is 3.2:1 by one source and 2.1:1 by another (SaaS Capital 2025); 3:1 is the common threshold — [Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/saas-startup-metrics-statistics-2026); [Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/saas-unit-economics).
- SMB CAC payback is 8.6 months (median, SaaS Hero) or 12–15 months (attributed to OpenView 2025) — [ScaleXP](https://www.scalexp.com/blog/saas-benchmark/cac-cac-payback/); [Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/startup-cac-payback-period-benchmarks-2026).

### Inferences (illustrative model, not data)
- Gallery at ₪390/month, 4% monthly churn → average life ≈ 25 months → revenue LTV ≈ ₪9,750. At 80% gross margin (assumed, to verify against actual hosting and processing costs) → ~₪7,800. For LTV:CAC ≥ 3, CAC must stay **≤ ₪2,600 per gallery**, which allows founder-led sales and in-person visits but not paid advertising. Payback at ₪390 × 0.8 = ₪312/month → ₪2,600 CAC pays back in ~8 months, within the benchmark.
- Per-exhibition at ₪590 with 6 shows a year → ₪3,540/gallery/year if every show is bought. Expect 2–3 in practice, so ~₪1,200–1,800/year. Margin depends heavily on capture cost: if the team sends someone to photograph, travel and labour can eat most of ₪590.

### Gaps
- ChartMogul, KeyBanc and OpenView primary reports were not fetched; figures come via aggregators. No art-specific SaaS churn data was found. Artsy's gallery churn is unknown.
- Not found or not checked in this pass: municipal or institution licence prices, ticketing (Israeli market), sponsorship benchmarks, print-on-demand margins, and art-data business precedents (Artnet's price database is one, but its segment revenue was not retrieved).
