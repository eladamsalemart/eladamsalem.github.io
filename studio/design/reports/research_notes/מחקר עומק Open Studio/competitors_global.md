# Global competitive landscape for OPEN STUDIO (research notes, 2026-10-28)

Scope: competitors and adjacent players worldwide, beyond the already-known set (Kunstmatrix, Artsteps, ArtRabbit, MyGallery3D, CUR8, Arte, Artland, Artsy, Matterport). Searches run in English, Japanese, Korean, Chinese, German, French and Hebrew. Dates in brackets are the date of the source or event. Where a fact could not be confirmed it is in Gaps, not in findings.

## 1. Exhibition discovery and gallery-guide apps by region

### Takeaway
Every major art city has a listings or guide product, but almost all are either a city calendar run by a magazine or gallery association (Tokyo Art Beat, Berlin's LVBG calendar, L'Officiel in Paris, Time Out in Tel Aviv), or a museum-audio-guide platform (Smartify, Bloomberg Connects). Traction numbers are small for the listings apps, and the guide platforms that scaled did it by selling to or being funded for institutions, not by charging visitors. No dedicated Hebrew gallery-map app turned up in search.

### Cited Findings

- Tokyo Art Beat (Art Beat, Inc.): bilingual Tokyo listings. AppBrain shows roughly 40K Google Play downloads for its one Android app (figure undated) — [AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/dev/Art+Beat%2C+Inc.+%28Japan%29/). US App Store page shows only 2 ratings — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id354579592).

MuPon had 18,000+ downloads in its first year (2010) — [AQ case study](https://www.aqworks.com/work/mupon).
- TAB ended its "myTAB" services when it launched a new app in Nov 2017 — [TAB, Nov 2017](https://tokyoartbeat.com/en/articles/-/a-new-tokyo-art-beat-app-and-the-end-of-mytab-services). - Search in Hebrew and English found no dedicated app that maps Tel Aviv or Israeli gallery shows. - Erev Rav (ערב רב), founded 2010 and edited by Yonatan Amir and Ronen Eidelman, is an independent journal of art, culture and society that also runs events. Search found no exhibitions-calendar feature — [Erev Rav](https://erev-rav.com/archives/english).

### Inferences
- Visitor-facing listings apps are a thin business. TAB's tens of thousands of downloads after 15+ years, Artmap's thin ratings, and See Saw's move to charging galleries all point the same way: revenue comes from venues or sponsors, not visitors.
- The guide platforms that scaled (Smartify 2M+ users, Bloomberg Connects 1,000+ institutions) are B2B2C museum tools. Bloomberg Connects is free to institutions, which makes it very hard to charge venues for a "guide" alone.
- Israel looks like genuine white space for a mobile-first, map-based, Hebrew/English gallery guide. That is an absence in search results, not proof, and should be checked in the Israeli app stores.
- China's fragmentation (many apps, plus WeChat mini-programs and Xiaohongshu discovery) suggests the social layer, not a listings database, is what holds audiences there.

## 2. Virtual exhibition and 3D tour providers: survivors and casualties

### Takeaway
The COVID-era virtual-exhibition wave split into three groups: (a) SaaS builders that survive at low price points ($12/month class) on education and small galleries (Kunstmatrix, Artsteps, V21); (b) companies that pivoted to retail or brands with venture money (Emperia to virtual stores, Spatial from VR meetings to NFT galleries); and (c) high-end gallery XR apps that went quiet after 2021 (Vortic). Search found no public evidence of a 3D-tour product that is generated automatically from phone photos and placed inside a consumer discovery app.

### Cited Findings
- **Online-sales context:** in 2020, dealers' online share of revenue tripled from 13% to 39%, art-fair online viewing rooms made up 9% of dealer sales, and online took 25% of the whole market by value, up from 9% in 2019 — [Art Basel/UBS Art Market Report 2021](https://www.artbasel.com/news/Art-Market-Report-2021).
- **Vortic** (London, founded by Oliver Miro, son of Victoria Miro): VR/AR exhibition app sold to galleries on subscription — [Art Critique, Mar 2020](https://www.art-critique.com/en/2020/03/vortic-to-release-next-month/); [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/new-extended-reality-app-1812807). It hosted The London Collective (20+ galleries, 2021) — [Artlyst](https://artlyst.com/?p=72373) and powered theVOV recreations of landmark exhibitions (Apr 2021) — [Museums Association](https://www.museumsassociation.org/museums-journal/news/2021/04/landmark-exhibitions-of-the-past-resurrected-on-xr-platform/). No coverage after 2021 was found.
- **V21 Artspace** (V21 Productions Ltd, UK, incorporated 22 Dec 2017, active): says 300+ 3D virtual exhibition tours, many free in a public archive, and pitches them as a complement to physical shows — [V21 About](https://v21artspace.com/about); [Endole](https://open.endole.co.uk/insight/brand/179627-v21-artspace).
- **Emperia** (London, launched 2019): raised a $10M Series A in Jan 2023 (Base10 lead; Sony Innovation Fund, Daphni and others). Positioned as "virtual retail" for fashion and art, with clients including Christie's and Getty Images — [UKTN, Jan 2023](https://www.uktech.news/fashion-tech/emperia-series-a-20230125); [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2023/01/201638-london-based-emperia-announces-10m-series-a-funding/); [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.com/news/business/emperia-raises-10-million-dollars-to-back-virtual-retail-solutions/2023012551884).
- **Spatial**: pivoted from enterprise AR/VR meetings to NFT galleries and "the metaverse for cultural events"; said 80% of users came from web and mobile — [Cheddar](https://www.cheddar.com/media/spatial-pivots-business-to-become-metaverse-for-cultural-events/); [TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/83429/spatial-abandons-vr-meetings-dives-headfirst-into-nfts/images/applenews-v4.png).
- **Oncyber**: free 3D/VR spaces for showing NFTs — [Startups Spain](https://startups.one.gob.es/companies/cyber_2_4). Its 2024–26 status is unknown.
- **Kunstmatrix**: plans from about $12/month (third party) — [ArtPlacer](https://www.artplacer.com/?p=9102); [Luminous Landscape](https://luminous-landscape.com/projects-and-presentation-using-a-virtual-gallery-to-display-photographs/). It also sells a separate edu product at 15/30/100/400 per month — [kunstmatrix.edu](https://edu.kunstmatrix.com/en) and a "digital twin of your gallery space" service — [Kunstmatrix](https://kunstmatrix.com/en/node/301).
- **Artsteps** (Dataverse Ltd, Greece, founded 2007): claims "tens of thousands" of exhibitions published (undated vendor claim) and is heavily used in education — [Artsteps blog](https://blog.artsteps.com/about); [Digital Orientalist, 2023](https://digitalorientalist.com/2023/02/14/an-alternative-to-the-undergraduate-research-paper-online-exhibitions-with-artsteps/).
- **Artland** (Copenhagen): $1M seed in Mar 2018 with 18,000 registered users at the time — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/03/13/artland-closes-1m-seed-for-its-social-art-marketplace/). Its 3D recordings let galleries archive shows permanently (2019) — [Artfix Daily](https://artfixdaily.com/artwire/release/121-artland-with-its-3d-online-art-galleries-enters-new-york-in-indepe). The Art Newspaper (Aug 2020) called its VR "clearly an advance" — [TAN](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2020/08/07/vr-vast-data-collection-and-some-great-art-untitled-art-online-fair-and-artland). No funding after 2018 was found.

### Inferences
- The lesson from the graveyard: 3D tours sold as a stand-alone substitute for visiting (the 2020 OVR logic) lost demand once fairs reopened. Survivors reframed tours as an archive or complement (V21, Artland) or sell to education (Artsteps, Kunstmatrix edu).
- The money moved to retail (Emperia). Art-only 3D players stayed small and cheap, so the per-gallery price ceiling for 3D documentation is low (about $12–$40 a month self-serve), unless capture is done for the gallery as a service.
- OPEN STUDIO's edge, automatic capture from phone photos, works against the main cost of the category (manual building or a Matterport scan). It fits the "permanent archive of a real show" framing that survived.

### Gaps
- Not verified: the status of Vortic after 2021 (whether it shut down or went dormant), Exhibbit, Hauntd, Artrendex, Seezeum, Exhibit.so, Oncyber after 2023, 3DVista pricing.
- No 2023–2025 Art Basel/UBS figures on online-viewing-room usage were retrieved.

## 3. Artist-centric platforms

### Takeaway
Artist-side tools are either inventory SaaS (Artwork Archive, $6–$42 a month) or curated marketplaces taking commission (Singulart, with the biggest venture backing). The newest social challenger, Cara, shows that artists will move fast for an artist-first, anti-AI network, but it is volunteer-run and unfunded. Nobody here combines an artist journal or behind-the-scenes clips with real exhibition documentation and visitor discovery.

### Cited Findings
- **Artwork Archive**: artist plans are reported at Apprentice $9/month annual or $10 monthly (100 pieces), Professional $18/$21 (500 pieces), Master $36/$42 — [Fitgap](https://us.fitgap.com/products/023961/artwork-archive). Capterra lists $8/$16/$24, with collector/artist plans from $6 and organisation plans from $24 — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/169087/Artwork-Archive/). The sources conflict.
- **Singulart** (Paris): €60M Series B in Nov 2021 led by Vitruvian Partners, with Ventech, Bpifrance and Turenne; described as the largest raise in the cultural sector — [Private Equity Wire](https://www.privateequitywire.co.uk/2021/11/18/309458/singulart-raises-eur60m-series-b-round). In 2025 it had about 12,000 artists from 165+ countries and sold about 1,700 works a month — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singulart). Revenue estimates conflict ($5–25M per Owler vs $77.6M per Growjo, neither reported by the company) — [Growjo](https://growjo.com/company/Singulart); [Owler](https://www.owler.com/company/singulart).
- **Cara**: founded by photographer Zhang Jingna as an AI-free space for human artists. Run by her and volunteers, it has avoided venture funding (status: active) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cara_(app)). It surged by about 600–650K users in a week in June 2024, after Meta's AI-training policy change (forum and podcast summaries, secondary) — [Podwise, Sep 2024](https://podwise.ai/episodes/1976009).
- **Artfinder** (UK): still operating. New-artist applications were paused "due to high volume" and set to reopen 1 Dec 2025 — [Artfinder](https://artfinder.com/sell). It restructured in 2020 ("Artfinder is changing") — [Artfinder blog](https://www.artfinder.com/blog/post/artfinder-is-changing-june2020/). The brief's "closed?" is not supported.
- **Sedition** (digital art editions): still active. It partnered with LG in Feb 2026 to bring 300+ curated digital works to LG Gallery+ TVs — [LG Newsroom](https://www.lg.com/global/newsroom/news/media-entertainment-solution/lg-gallery-presents-digital-artwork-from-sedition-including-select-exclusive-pieces/).

### Inferences
- Cara's spike proves artists will move for values (no AI, artist-first). A "Spotify for visual art" that is visibly artist-first and AI-free could borrow that positioning, but Cara also shows a spike does not equal retained MAU (unverified).
- Artists already pay $10–40 a month for inventory tools, so a paid artist tier (portfolio plus 3D documentation of every show) is a realistic price anchor.

### Gaps
- Not verified: Saatchi Art, Artmajeur, Partner in Art, Patreon artist metrics, ArtStation (Epic), Vero, Glass, DailyArt user numbers; current Cara MAU.

## 4. Collector and marketplace players, and the graveyards

### Takeaway
The big collector platforms consolidated under one financial owner in 2025–26 (Artsy plus Artnet under Beowolff Capital), followed by deep layoffs. Masterworks reports continuing operating losses. The best-known failures (Paddle8, Auctionata) died from cash and trust problems, not lack of interest. The collector/transaction layer is crowded and contracting, so it is a poor entry point for a discovery-first startup.

### Cited Findings
- **Artsy and Artnet**: Beowolff Capital took a controlling stake in Artsy in 2025, made a takeover offer for Artnet AG on 27 May 2025 (about $65M), and Artnet was delisted from Frankfurt on 22 Aug 2025 — [Art Law clippings](https://itsartlaw.org/al-clippings/artsy-and-artnet-merger/); [Artribune](https://www.artribune.com/?p=1160595). The two merged under CEO Jeffrey Yin. Dozens were laid off on 16 Apr 2026 (including senior Artnet News reporters) and Artnet's Berlin office closed — [Hyperallergic, Apr 2026](https://hyperallergic.com/art-movements-dozens-laid-off-at-artnet-and-artsy/); [Artforum](https://www.artforum.com/news/bloodbath-follows-artsy-artnet-consolidation-1234748056/). Artnet revenue fell 12% in H1 2025 — [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/art-movements-dozens-laid-off-at-artnet-and-artsy/); The Art Newspaper questioned the merger's viability (23 Apr 2026) — [TAN](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/04/23/comment-%7C-how-viable-is-the-artnet-and-artsys-merger).
- **Masterworks**: SEC filings say it has had operating losses and negative operating cash flow since 2022. Funded by founder loans, about $110M of private equity (Oct 2021) and a $25M credit line (expiring Nov 2025, undrawn as of 26 Sep 2025). Art transaction volumes "continued to reset" from COVID highs through H1 2025 — [SEC Form 1-SA, 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1894989/000149315225015345/form1-sa.htm).
- **Paddle8**: parent Auctionata Paddle8 went insolvent in 2017 when investors would not fund it — [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/auctionata-insolvency-proceedings-822965?amp=1). Paddle8 filed Chapter 11 in March 2020 after suits over unremitted charity-auction funds; the trustee alleged client money was used for operating costs — [The Art Newspaper, Mar 2020](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/online-auction-house-paddle8-files-for-bankruptcy); [Artforum](https://www.artforum.com/news/paddle8-bankruptcy-trustee-launches-6-million-suit-against-former-member-of-board-of-directors-84004).
- **Artspace**: founded 2011, bought by Phaidon (Leon Black) in Aug 2014, later cut editorial staff — [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industrynews/publisher-news/article/63666-phaidon-acquires-artspace.html); [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/artspace-layoff-editorial-808346?amp=1). At the time, Artnet called the sale a sign of an "inevitable shake-out in online art sales" — [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/market/artspace-sale-augurs-inevitable-shake-out-in-online-art-sales-78371?amp=1).

### Inferences
- The graveyard pattern: transaction platforms die on cash handling, thin margins or a market reset (Paddle8, Auctionata), and the survivors consolidate (Artsy and Artnet). Discovery and editorial are cost centres being cut inside the merged group, which leaves room for a lean, community-driven discovery layer.

### Gaps
- Not verified: "Exhibition.app" (no source found); Artsy traffic and revenue for 2025–26; Saatchi Art ownership changes.

## 5. Synthesis: white space and graveyards

### Takeaway
Among the roughly 40 players reviewed, none combines (1) map-based discovery of current physical shows, (2) automatically generated 3D documentation of those real shows, and (3) an artist "studio journal" feed with follow. Each exists alone: discovery (TAB, Artmap, See Saw, Ocula), 3D (Artland, V21, Kunstmatrix, Matterport), artist social (Cara, Instagram). Artland came closest (social app plus 3D gallery recordings), but has shown no growth signal since its 2018 seed.

### Cited Findings
- Artland is the nearest precedent: a social art marketplace app (2018) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/03/13/artland-closes-1m-seed-for-its-social-art-marketplace/) with permanent 3D archives of gallery shows — [Artfix Daily](https://artfixdaily.com/artwire/release/121-artland-with-its-3d-online-art-galleries-enters-new-york-in-indepe).
- Community and social layers retain users in other markets: VART and 在艺 lead Chinese show apps by rating count — [App Store VART](https://apps.apple.com/cn/app/id881580775); [App Store 在艺](https://apps.apple.com/cn/app/id1059667773).
- Free institutional competition: Bloomberg Connects gives guides away to 1,000+ institutions — [bloombergconnects.org](https://bloombergconnects.org/).

### Inferences
- **White space:** (a) Israel has no mobile gallery map or guide app found; (b) automatic 3D from phone photos as a permanent archive of every show, tied to the artist's profile; (c) an artist-first, AI-free journal feed connected to real-world shows.
- **Graveyards to avoid:** 3D tours sold as a stand-alone replacement for visiting (post-2021 OVR decline, Vortic gone quiet); transaction or auction businesses holding client money (Paddle8); visitor-paid listings (thin downloads everywhere); ad and editorial-heavy models (Artsy/Artnet layoffs).
- **Monetisation lessons:** venues pay for listings (See Saw), institutions pay or are subsidised for guides (Smartify, Bloomberg), artists pay small SaaS fees (Artwork Archive, Kunstmatrix). Charging visitors has little precedent beyond TAB's membership.

### Gaps
- Downloads, MAU and revenue are missing for most players. Crunchbase, Sensor Tower and data.ai were not accessed.
- Several named players (Exhibbit, Hauntd, Artrendex, Seezeum, Exhibit.so, Kunstaspekte, Slash Paris, Artips, Arte Informado, Art Fund app, Vero, Glass, DailyArt, Saatchi Art, Artmajeur, Patreon) were not researched within the call budget and need a follow-up pass.
