# The owner's standard: read before any change, apply before showing anything

Built 23.11 from every message the owner has sent about the app (11.03 to 11.23). Each rule here is something he
had to say more than once, or a miss that a professional would not have made. The goal: he never corrects the same
kind of thing twice. When he corrects something new, add it here the same day, as a general rule, with its date.

## The five habits behind every miss so far (check yourself against them before you start)

1. **Built the literal words, not their world.** Ask what the request means in a real gallery, museum or pro tool, and
   do all of it. "Join Ritual to the museum" also means: through its own arches, at full quality, with its text on a wall.
2. **Fixed one surface, not the one shared model.** The artist's editor, the published show, Home, the map, the
   museum and the control room read one object. Change it at the source, then check every place it shows.
3. **Stopped at "it renders", not "it would stand in a real building".** Check the physics: nothing floats, nothing
   passes through anything, every opening leads somewhere, nothing is half-done.
4. **Shipped a tool without a professional's checklist.** Before building an editor, list what a curator, architect
   or Photoshop user expects (below), and build those in the first version.
5. **Reported "done" without walking the whole chain.** Walk it as a visitor and as the artist, end to end, on a
   phone, and look at the result. A check that passes, or a sub-session's report, is not proof.

## Rules by theme (ordered by how often he had to say them)

**One source of truth (8 times).** What an artist edits, what is published and what Home, the map and the museum
show must be one and the same: same works, same pillars and angles, same text, same publish state. No duplicates, no
ghost "untitled" copies. A real show never lists as virtual, or the other way round. Test the full chain before
reporting: save, send for approval, approve in the control room, see it on the artist page, Home, the map and the
museum. (Gaga out of sync and Ritual twice, 16.11. The 2 pillars were published as 1, 21.11. Ritual and the
Renaissance show missing from the museum, 22.11.)

**Realism is the bar, measured against a real photo (8 times).** "Fine for 3D" is not the bar. Compare with a real
place or photo: textures (concrete, plaster, wood, floor), light, sky, plants, reflections, wall thickness. "Digital"
and "flat" are the worst words. Raise the bar yourself. A room, a show or an outside joins the museum only at the
best level already reached anywhere in the app, never below it. (Concrete, 3.11. Flat nature, 8.11. "15%, aim for
100", 16.11. Ritual unlike its original, 22.11.)
Memory is saved where the visitor isn't looking, never where they are: far things are held small and come back at
full sharpness as the visitor nears them; measure the start view AND the inside of the newest room. (23.11: artists'
~2000 px uploads held in full on a phone filled 438 of 643 MB; now 768 px from afar, full up close: 386 MB.)
Sharpness is measured, not guessed: pixels per metre on a wall against the room's own tour. (Ritual in the museum,
23.11: a phone cut its 14 m walls to 73 px/m against the tour's 200. Walls now keep 2048 px.)

**Physically possible and finished (7 times).** It must work as a real building would:
- Pedestals only indoors. Plants on the ground. Works on walls. Signs on posts or walls.
- A joined exhibition keeps its own architecture. Its real arches and doors are the passages to the museum. No
  opening is blocked or strange: it leads somewhere, or a wall closes it and looks built.
- Wall text sits flush on the wall, inside its bounds, and is seen correct from an angle. "Clear" means really
  clear: letters on the wall, no sheet, no thickness.
- An opening made walkable keeps the wall's real thickness: its sides end flush with the outer wall, never
  sticking out of it or leaving a gap. Look at every opening from outside, at an angle.
- One wall, one thickness: every opening is cut through the whole wall in its real shape (an arch as an arch), with
  both jambs and a head at that depth. Never a rectangle cut in front of an arch, a missing jamb, or a slab between
  openings. No visible seams or ghost planes: a texture tile's edge or a stray surface is a bug. (Ritual, 23.11.)
- Nothing cut off. Panels that need room open full screen. The camera never jumps by itself.
(Pedestals outdoors, 9.11 and 10.11. Text half in the air, 21.11 and 23.11. Entry through a painting, then a blocked
arch, 22.11 and 23.11.)

**Editors work like a pro tool (7 times).** Every item that exists can be selected, moved, rotated at any angle,
resized, duplicated, removed and added back, with undo. A plan view from above. Equal spacing to walls and pillar
ends. A levelled laser line whose height can be set, toggled, and seen across the space. Zoom, and a quick look at
the work in the space. Number fields for exact size and height. Big, findable buttons, never tiny hidden ones.

**If it exists, the artist can change it (6 times).** Nothing pre-selected that can't be changed. No dead ends: each
thing in the space is reachable from the editor or the plan.

**The UI explains itself (5 times).** It separates and explains real exhibitions and virtual ones, published and not
published, what On View means, and why a tour needs many photos. Two clear paths: artist and art lover.

**Don't touch what he approved. Keep it compact (5 times).** Home's look is fixed unless he asks in words. Simple and
small beats big: when he says "smaller", make it clearly smaller. No clutter. Ask before changing a look.

**English app, Hebrew to him.** The app is English everywhere except the owner's control room. Hebrew elsewhere
is a bug. Every report to him is in Hebrew.

**Real content only.** Real artists, works allowed for use, real exhibitions, a real map, real days and hours.
Never invented ones.

**Works of art.** Never crop a painting or change its proportions. White margins on his photos are intentional.
Show works at their real size, lit as in a gallery.

## His taste

- The museum is a closed building with real walls, skylights and some windows. Its layout is asymmetric and open,
  not too neat or symmetric. "Under one sky": soft light from the round skylights. The transitions between
  exhibitions have colour and creativity. Outside: tropical, rich, real.
- Colour is painterly, pastel, with a brush-stroke feel, never flat coloured circles. He likes elegant black
  poster looks.
- The hand-written "my open studio", the round window, text that looks pasted on a wall.
- He rejects text that sounds like AI.

## How he wants to work

- Big design choices: research first, then 3 to 5 distinct directions (a page or PDF), and he picks. Small ones:
  decide yourself.
- Ask in a separate popup with choices, never inside a long message. Report short, in Hebrew, and say what was not
  checked.
- Work alone and long, nights included. Resume after a quota reset unasked. Do the chores yourself (tools,
  downloads), never send him on them.
- One main session leads (the "Open studio" folder). Sub-sessions take parallel parts, never the same files, and
  their results are checked before they reach him.
- Approval gates before anything goes live.

## Before calling anything done

1. Name the real-world version of the request (a gallery, a museum, a pro tool) and list what it implies. Do all of it.
2. Change the shared model, then open every surface that shows it.
3. Walk it at 390×844 as a visitor, from the way a visitor arrives (Home, the map, the museum door). Look at each new
   thing from eye height, close, far and from an angle. Walk the artist's chain end to end.
4. Physics pass: nothing floats, nothing passes through anything, every opening leads somewhere, every panel fits.
5. Quality pass: put it beside the best room in the app. If it is worse, it isn't done.
6. Read your diff for anything removed by accident.
7. Report in Hebrew what you saw working, with one shot, and say plainly what was not checked.
